# Medical Considerations

## I am not a doctor.
But I might suggest one, if I notice something of concern.
I also might suggest a therapist, depending on what comes up during the program.

## On the matter of vocal surgery:
Periodically someone asks me about this.

As far as I'm concerned, surgery is a personal decision for those who choose to undergo it.
The vast majority can achieve far beyond their voice goals through training alone,
but even I recognize that some minority of people can expect a benefit from the right choice of procedure.
Even so, people who go through vocal surgery generally still need to train some aspects of their new voice.
[Here's a video interview with someone who had the surgery.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0j614_-mXaY)

Training comes with neither a convalescent period nor the risk of surgical complications.
With training, you can easily adjust your sound at will,
even to the point of deliberately "turning off" your modified voice - a power some people might need.

Regardless of your decision on the matter, I will respect you.
However, if you have had vocal surgery, or if you are contemplating it,
I must ask you to disclose that fact. It will influence the nature and extent of what I can do with you.

## Hormones:

**Testosterone's** effects may not all happen at the same speed or timing, which can lead to voice cracking and the like.
Additionally, not everyone taking T for transition intuitively gains access to the full suite of changes.
Voice training remains a valuable adjunct to whatever the hormones do for you.

**Estrogen** does not reverse the changes caused by testosterone.

Regardless, you can train your voice to sound typical of either masculine or feminine presentation,
or indeed any mishmash of the two if that's your bag.
