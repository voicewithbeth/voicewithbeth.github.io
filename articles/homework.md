Homework is "assigned" in the sense that if you do it, I think it will help you.
I won't force you to do it, but regular daily *practice* makes regular daily *progress.*

When I ask for recordings, that generally means I plan to spend a few minutes to review those recordings and offer feedback.
It may not happen the same day, but it's the plan. That can enable early course corrections and help prevent missteps.

Please reach out if you have technical difficulties doing the homework.

Lastly, please do not treat the homework as an obstacle. Life happens.
If the homework doesn't get done, so be it. You're still invited to come to the next lesson.

(assuming your bills are paid, that is.)

# General Structure of Homework:

## Free Flying Fancy

Most of the time, I'll ask you to practice your skills *playfully* for a few minutes at a time, ideally 5-10 times per day.
This is based on notions around skill acquisition and retention.

## Recorded Experimentation

Sometimes, I'll ask you to make recordings of yourself exploring an attribute of your voice.
I'll ask that you send me the following as a direct message:

- Text: explaining what your *intention* is for the practice session
- Sound: The most relevant 30-60 seconds of *the actual recording*
- Text: Your own *evaluation* of the result
- Text: Your *plans* for the following day

## Community Integration

There's no way around it: If you want to master a skill, you have to *use the skill* for a meaningful purpose.
As you gain the physical skills to coordinate a new voice, I'll ask you to use it with greater frequency and in more diverse situations.
You'll want to make some plans in advance around high, medium, and low-stakes situations.
Naturally, you can practice in lower stakes situations first, then medium, and finally the high-stakes things.

Low-stakes might be friends and accomplices who will implicitly support your vocal transformation.
This could also include random strangers who will have no prior expectations around how you sound.
Here, the idea is to go all-out and use everything you know.

Medium-stakes might be transactional meetings where you need to be understood, but there's little risk of long-term issues.
These are appropriate when you have good strategies and the self-awareness to fix yourself when something *begins* to go sideways.
While you're still gaining mastery, these interactions might call for you to focus on one or perhaps two skills.
You'll also want to have good recovery tools in place.

High-stakes interactions -- the kind where you can't afford to slip -- might best be saved for when you're more confident.
You'll know when this is. You may find a way to still practice some small feature that won't lead to trouble.

As you gain mastery over time, you'll be able to use more skills in more places with confidence. Do it.

Finally, some people have the misfortune to have people they just can't afford to use a new voice around *ever.*
If this is you, then, well, put your personal safety first.
