People sometimes ask, "Do I pass?" Or perhaps the question comes phrased as "Do I sound like `$GENDER`"?

## Here's why I try to avoid gendered "pass/fail" feedback:

In general, gender-oriented feedback is not actionable.

A self-respecting vocal gender modification program does not simply walk you in a straight line from masculine to feminine (or the reverse). If it were that easy, people wouldn't need coaching. Instead, we break it down into actionable parts. You'll learn to

1. Adjust specific vocal qualities,
2. Coordinate those adjustments to get into the neighborhood of your target voice, and finally
3. Fine-tune them to precisely resemble the voice you have chosen for yourself.

The important question, then, is not whether you "sound like `$GENDER`" in some absolute sense.
It's whether, for each vocal quality, you have the knobs turned too low, too high, or just right.
That kind of feedback will enable you to make forward progress.
And incidentally, that is why it's so important to train yourself to hear and evaluate these same qualities in real time.
Once you're confident in your own ears, you'll never need to ask whether your voice is on target.

## Passing is in the ear of the beholder.

There are cultural aspects to "passing", and there are dangers to passing judgement on the gender of someone's voice. The point is not hug-boxing; it's the recognition that standards and social pressures differ across space and time, as do people's responses to them. Voices are (quite clearly) extremely malleable, but societies fail to recognize this.

Accordingly, speech communities apply subtle and sometimes overt pressure for people to conform to local stereotypes: Both men and women *tend,* most of the time, to imitate those around them who they want to be accepted and respected by. If that means holding a different vocal posture *without necessarily being aware of the fact,* then over time it will slowly creep into someone's default behavior.

For example, I have met a decent number of trans women from other countries who very clearly get all the social signals of passing *locally* (e.g. being frau(line) on the telephone) but my General-American ears want to hear a different configuration to be quite convinced on an instinctive level. Meanwhile some of us North American girls probably sound positively squeaky in comparison.

Passing is not something you do. It's something done to you.
